Investment Thesis

We find markets where operators are still running on Excel.

Most vertical SaaS opportunities aren't in Silicon Valley — they're in the unglamorous, overlooked industries that employ millions of Europeans and still manage everything with spreadsheets, WhatsApp, and phone calls.

We build the software they never had. Then we add the marketplace layer that makes both sides of the market depend on us. That's how you build a moat.

01
Fragmented, unsexy markets
If a market is boring enough that no VC has touched it, that's a signal. No competition, desperate operators, and a clear path to category ownership.
02
SaaS first, marketplace second
Charge operators for software that saves them hours per week. The marketplace layer then locks in both sides and creates network effects money can't buy.
03
Build before raising
We bootstrap to proof. Revenue, retention, NPS — then we raise from a position of strength. Giving away 25% of something unproven is a tax on impatience.
04
Netherlands first, then Europe
Dominate one market deeply before expanding. A platform with 90% penetration in one country is more valuable than 5% everywhere.

How we work

The build playbook.

01
Find the Excel market
We look for operators with 10–500 customers who still manage everything manually. No good software exists. The market is big enough to matter, small enough that no VC has noticed.
02
Build the software
We build lean, focused operator software that solves the 3–5 core problems. Agenda. Waitlist. Invoicing. Contracts. Make it so good they can't imagine going back to Excel.
03
Add the marketplace
Once operators are locked in by software, we add consumer-facing search and discovery. Now both sides of the market need us. Network effects compound. Churn becomes structurally impossible.
04
Prove before raising
We bootstrap until the numbers tell the story. €10k MRR, 90%+ retention, NPS above 50. Then we raise — or we sell. Either way we negotiate from strength, not hope.
05
Expand the stack
Payments, insurance, financing, logistics — every adjacent service we add increases ARPU and deepens lock-in. The goal is to become the operating system for the vertical.
06
Cross the border
Netherlands first. Then Belgium, Germany, France. The playbook is the same in every market. Local sales, local operators, same product. Repeatability is the moat.
